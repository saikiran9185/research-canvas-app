# The Research Canvas name

The **code** is AGPL-3.0-or-later — copy it, change it, ship it, fork it. This
page is only about the **name**, which the licence does not cover.

## The short version

- **Use the software freely.** The licence already grants you that, and this
  page takes nothing away from it.
- **Say true things freely.** "Built on Research Canvas", "compatible with
  Research Canvas", "a fork of Research Canvas", "I contribute to Research
  Canvas" — all fine, no permission needed.
- **Don't name your fork after ours.** If you distribute a modified version,
  give it your own name. Otherwise people can't tell whose bugs they're
  reporting, and the name stops meaning anything.
- **Don't imply endorsement** you don't have.

If you're not sure, open a discussion and ask. The answer is usually yes.

## Why the name is held separately

The project is founder-led and community-built. The code belongs to its
contributors and cannot be captured. The **name** is what lets people know
they're getting the real thing, maintained by the people who say they maintain
it — so it is stewarded by the founder,
[@saikiran9185](https://github.com/saikiran9185), on the project's behalf.

Holding the name is a responsibility, not a revenue stream. It will never be
used to charge for the software, to restrict who may use it, or to stop anyone
forking the code.

## Status

**Research Canvas** is currently an unregistered mark used by the project.

An honest note for contributors and for the founder: "Research Canvas" is a
descriptive name — two ordinary words describing what the tool does. Descriptive
marks are difficult to register and difficult to defend anywhere. If the project
ever needs a name it can actually protect, that means choosing a distinctive one,
and the earlier that happens the cheaper it is. This is recorded here so the
decision is made deliberately rather than discovered late.

Nothing on this page is legal advice.
